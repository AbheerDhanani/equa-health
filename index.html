Quality & Science

Inside the Medicine: What Are You Actually Swallowing?

Every pharmaceutical tablet consists of two components. Understanding them removes 90% of all doubts about generics.

A

1. The Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient (API)

This is the active chemical molecule that heals: it dilates blood vessels, kills bacteria, or regulates glucose. By law, a generic must contain the exact same API in the exact same dosage as the branded original.

B

2. Inactive Excipients (Binders & Coating)

These are pharmaceutical-grade cornstarches, lactose, or cellulose that hold the tablet shape and dissolve smoothly in stomach acid. They do not alter the medicinal effect.

C

3. Bioequivalence Testing

Before approval by government authorities (such as CDSCO or US FDA), the generic must prove that it delivers the drug into the human bloodstream at the exact same rate and extent as the originator.

The Pharmacist's Desk

Plain Answers to Honest Questions

Calm, patient explanations without medical jargon—just like a pharmacist speaking with an elderly family member.

Why do generic tablets sometimes look different in color or shape from the brand I’m used to?

Under trademark and patent law, pharmaceutical companies can trademark the specific shape, dye color, or packaging style of a pill—even after the patent on the medicine itself expires. Therefore, another manufacturer must use a different harmless food-grade dye or round shape. The active therapeutic molecule inside is identical.

If generic medicines are so good, why doesn't my doctor always write them?

Doctors see dozens of patients every day and write prescriptions under immense time pressure. They naturally recall the brand names they learned in medical school or saw on the sample boxes left by pharmaceutical representatives. When you politely ask your doctor for the generic salt name, nearly all doctors agree immediately because they want their patients to be able to afford their medications.

How do I know the factory that made this generic medicine maintains clean, strict standards?

India is the pharmacy of the world, supplying over 40% of all generic drugs used in the United States and Europe. EQUA medicines are sourced solely from facilities certified by the World Health Organization (WHO-GMP) and the CDSCO. The very same assembly lines that package expensive brand-name tablets often package the generic equivalents on the adjacent shift.

Is it safe to switch to a generic for lifelong conditions like high blood pressure or diabetes?

Yes. In fact, clinical studies show that patients taking generic medications for chronic conditions have higher treatment adherence and fewer hospital visits. Why? Because when a monthly prescription costs ₹300 instead of ₹1,800, patients never delay refills or skip doses to save money.

Why does EQUA never use the word "cheap"?

Because language matters. In everyday life, "cheap" suggests something made with shortcuts, corners cut, or inferior materials. Equal medicine is not a shortcut. It is the exact molecular standard, priced fairly because the original patent monopoly expired. We believe in fair price, never compromise.
